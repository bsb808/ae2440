Assignment Evaluation Framework

Intent

Assignments in this course are designed to build confidence and competence in applying programming to engineering problem solving. Each assignment includes several exercises, but only one representative exercise is graded for evaluation.

The primary goal is to encourage consistent engagement and improvement, not to penalize small mistakes. Grading emphasizes understanding, effort, and clarity of communication. Students who complete the work thoughtfully and demonstrate learning will typically earn A or B grades.

Grading rubric for the selected exercise

Criterion Weight A (Excellent) B (Satisfactory) Incomplete
Problem understanding and approach 25% Shows a clear understanding of the problem and applies an appropriate, well-reasoned method. Demonstrates general understanding; approach is reasonable but may not be the most efficient or fully explained. Misinterprets or only partially addresses the problem.
Implementation and code quality 35% Code runs correctly or nearly correctly; organized, readable, and follows MATLAB conventions. Code mostly works; minor syntax or logic errors but demonstrates solid effort and structure. Code incomplete or largely incorrect; intent unclear.
Testing and debugging effort 20% Demonstrates testing or checking of results; identifies or discusses potential issues. Some checking or validation attempted, though not systematic. No evidence of checking, testing, or debugging.
Presentation and communication 20% Output and plots are clear, labeled, and briefly explained following engineering conventions. Output mostly clear; minor labeling or explanation issues. Output unclear or missing explanation.

Grade scale (mastery-focused)

Score Grade Meaning
90–100 A Excellent mastery and presentation
80–89 B Proficient understanding with minor issues
below 80 Feedback only Encouraged to discuss or revise

Summary

This grading approach:

  • Rewards consistent effort and steady improvement.
  • Encourages sound engineering habits: testing, verification, and clear communication.
  • Keeps grading meaningful while reducing performance pressure.
  • Ensures every assignment contributes to the development of practical programming competence.
